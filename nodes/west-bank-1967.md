# West Bank Occupation: Permanent Provisional Status

The 1967 occupation was declared temporary. Nearly six decades later, 'temporary' has become the most permanent feature of the landscape. This isn't failure—it's the design.

The permit regime is necronomy infrastructure: Palestinians need permits to build, to farm, to move, to work. Each permit requires fees, applications, waiting periods, denials that generate appeals. An entire administrative apparatus profits from managing access to what was once simply lived.

Meanwhile, the security industry profits from managing the reaction to the permits. Checkpoints, barriers, surveillance systems, biometric databases—all justified by the 'temporary' crisis that must be managed indefinitely.

The necronomy completes when both sides develop economies dependent on the occupation's continuation. Aid flows to manage the humanitarian crisis. Defense contracts flow to manage the security crisis. Think tanks profit from analyzing the crisis. NGOs profit from documenting the crisis. 

Resolution would eliminate the revenue stream. Permanent provisional status is the business model.
