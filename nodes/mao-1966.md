# The Cultural Revolution: Revolution as Business Model

Mao's Cultural Revolution (1966-1976) wasn't about achieving communism—it was about institutionalizing permanent crisis as a governance mechanism. By declaring continuous revolution, Mao ensured the Party would always be necessary to manage the chaos it created.

The Red Guards weren't solving problems; they were generating demand for administrative intervention. Every struggle session, every denunciation, every forced confession created work for Party officials, required documentation, needed oversight.

The necronomy is visible in the structure: you can never finish revolutionizing. Any declaration of victory would eliminate the justification for Party control. So the revolution must be permanent, the enemy must be eternal, and the crisis must never resolve.

When the Cultural Revolution 'ended' in 1976, it didn't actually end—it just transformed. The mechanisms of surveillance, denunciation, and administrative control remained. The revolution became the market reform era, but the fundamental necronomy persisted: manufacture crisis, then profit from managing it.
