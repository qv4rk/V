# The PATRIOT Act: Emergency as Infrastructure

The USA PATRIOT Act was passed 45 days after 9/11 as emergency legislation. 'Temporary' provisions that were supposed to sunset have been renewed continuously for over two decades. The emergency became permanent because the infrastructure built around it is now too profitable to dismantle.

The necronomy is visible in the contractor ecosystem: surveillance technology companies, private security firms, biometric database providers, security screening operators, intelligence analysts, threat assessment consultants. Billions in contracts dependent on the permanent emergency.

The TSA alone costs $8 billion annually and employs over 60,000 people. Security theater that exists not to make flying safer, but to demonstrate that the crisis requires constant management. Any suggestion that the threat has diminished is met with new threat assessments justifying expanded screening.

The necronomy completes when the security measures themselves generate the need for more security. Every new screening technology creates new evasion techniques, requiring newer technology. Every expansion of surveillance creates new privacy concerns, requiring more oversight, requiring more administrators.

Victory in the 'war on terror' would be economic disaster for the industries built around fighting it. So the war continues, threats are perpetually discovered, and the emergency infrastructure becomes permanent.
