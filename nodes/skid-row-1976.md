# Skid Row Containment: The Nonprofit Necronomy

In 1976, the LAPD published the "Blue Book"—a policy guide designating a 50-block area in downtown LA as a containment zone for the homeless. Officers were instructed to actively shepherd unhoused people into this area and prevent them from dispersing to other neighborhoods.

The policy was never about solving homelessness. It was about managing visibility. Concentrate the problem in one place, then build an entire ecosystem around managing—not ending—that concentration.

Today, Skid Row generates hundreds of millions in annual revenue. Nonprofits, service providers, grant administrators, consultants, security contractors—all dependent on the problem persisting. Any actual solution would be a mass layoff.

The necronomy is in the design: the worse the crisis gets, the more funding flows in. Success is measured by the scale of intervention, not by outcomes. You can't disrupt a market that profits from its own failure to function.
