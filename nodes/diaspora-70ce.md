# The 70 CE Diaspora: Manufacturing Permanence

When Titus breached Jerusalem's walls in 70 CE, the genetic code of a people fractured into two branches. Branch A scattered into the diaspora, preserving identity through ritual and law. Branch B stayed, their Hebrew roots growing into the soil that would later bear Muslim and Christian fruit.

This wasn't just conquest. It was the creation of a permanent administrative problem. The scattered branch would spend millennia negotiating for return. The rooted branch would spend millennia defending against displacement. Both branches would generate endless revenue for mediators, administrators, legal scholars, and eventually, military contractors.

Genetic studies confirm what history obscured: Palestinians and Jews share the same Levantine foundation. The "Zionist" displacing a cousin. The "Resistance Fighter" striking a returned relative. The separation is cultural software running on shared biological hardware.

The necronomy begins not in the siege itself, but in the permanence of the split. No resolution. No reunion. Just endless administration of an unresolved wound that both sides have learned to monetize.
