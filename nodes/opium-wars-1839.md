# The Opium Wars: Addiction as Empire

Britain had a trade problem: they wanted Chinese tea and silk, but China wanted nothing Britain produced. The solution? Create artificial demand by flooding China with opium from British-controlled India.

When China tried to ban the drug, Britain went to war—twice—to force the market open. The Treaty of Nanking (1842) didn't just open ports; it institutionalized the addiction economy. China was required to accept opium imports indefinitely.

The necronomy operates on multiple levels: the drug trade itself, the military enforcement of market access, the administrative apparatus managing treaty compliance, the rehabilitation industry that emerges from the crisis, and eventually, the communist revolution that claims to solve the problem by creating an entirely new set of permanent administrative mechanisms.

This is imperial necronomy: don't just conquer territory—create dependency that requires endless management.
